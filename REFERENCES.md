# References

This file collects the academic papers that informed the project's
research into market microstructure, order flow, liquidity, momentum,
news, execution, change detection, point processes, and backtesting
methodology.

## Market Microstructure, Order Flow & Liquidity

1.  **Cont, R., Kukanov, A., & Stoikov, S. (2014).** *The Price Impact
    of Order Book Events.* Journal of Financial Econometrics, 12(1),
    47--88.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003\
    arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402\
    Relevant to order-flow imbalance (OFI), short-horizon price impact,
    and the relationship between order flow and market depth. The paper
    finds a strong short-horizon relationship between OFI and price
    changes, with impact inversely related to market depth.

2.  **Amihud, Y. (2002).** *Illiquidity and Stock Returns: Cross-Section
    and Time-Series Effects.* Journal of Financial Markets, 5(1),
    31--56.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1386-4181(01)00024-6\
    Relevant to liquidity measurement and the Amihud illiquidity ratio,
    particularly for small firms. 

3.  **Bildik, R. (2001).** *Intra-day Seasonalities on Stock Returns:
    Evidence from the Turkish Stock Market.* Emerging Markets Review,
    2(4), 387--417.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1566-0141(01)00026-7\
    Relevant to intraday seasonality, including the U-shaped/W-shaped
    behavior of returns, volume, volatility, and bid-ask spreads across
    the trading day. 

## Point Processes & Market Dynamics

4.  **Bacry, E., Mastromatteo, I., & Muzy, J.-F. (2015).** *Hawkes
    Processes in Finance.* arXiv:1502.04592.\
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04592\
    Review of Hawkes-process applications in high-frequency finance,
    including volatility, market stability, systemic risk, optimal
    execution, and order-book dynamics.

## Execution & Market Impact

5.  **Almgren, R., & Chriss, N. (2001).** *Optimal Execution of
    Portfolio Transactions.* Journal of Risk.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2001.041\
    Foundational work on the tradeoff between transaction costs, market
    impact, and volatility risk in execution. 

## Momentum & Price Continuation

6.  **Jegadeesh, N., & Titman, S. (1993).** *Returns to Buying Winners
    and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency.* The
    Journal of Finance, 48(1), 65--91.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x\
    Foundational momentum research documenting positive returns from
    buying past winners and selling past losers over 3--12 month holding
    periods.

7.  **George, T. J., & Hwang, C.-Y. (2004).** *The 52-Week High and
    Momentum Investing.* The Journal of Finance, 59(5), 2145--2176.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x\
    Relevant to momentum and the relationship between a stock's current
    price, its 52-week high, and subsequent returns.
    

## News, Attention & Information Timing

8.  **Barber, B. M., & Odean, T. (2008).** *All That Glitters: The
    Effect of Attention and News on the Buying Behavior of Individual
    and Institutional Investors.* The Review of Financial Studies,
    21(2), 785--818.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm079\
    Relevant to attention-driven buying, news, abnormal volume, and
    extreme one-day returns. 

9.  **Tetlock, P. C. (2011).** *All the News That's Fit to Reprint: Do
    Investors React to Stale Information?* The Review of Financial
    Studies, 24(5), 1481--1512.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhq141 
    Relevant to stale/repeated news, reduced responses to stale
    information, and subsequent return reversals. The commonly cited
    publication year is 2011, not 2008. 

10. **Kargarzadeh, A., Khaledian, N., Parvini, N., Ghatak, S., &
    Khaledian, A. (2026).** *Buy the Rumor, Sell the News: When Is News
    Priced In?* arXiv:2608.14014.\
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014
    Relevant to the timing of price discovery around financial news,
    including whether price movement occurs before, at, or after
    publication. The study analyzes 4.57 million financial news articles
    covering roughly 3,000 U.S. stocks. 

## Cryptocurrency & Behavioral Dynamics

11. **Bouri, E., Gupta, R., & Roubaud, D. (2018).** *Herding Behaviour
    in Cryptocurrencies.* Finance Research Letters, 29, 216--223.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2018.07.008
    Relevant to collective behavior and herding in cryptocurrency
    markets.

## Change Detection

12. **Lorden, G. (1971).** *Procedures for Reacting to a Change in
    Distribution.* The Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 42(6),
    1897--1908.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177693055
    Foundational work on sequential change detection and quickest
    detection procedures.

13. **Moustakides, G. V. (1986).** *Optimal Stopping Times for Detecting
    Changes in Distributions.* The Annals of Statistics, 14(4),
    1379--1387.\
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176350164
    Establishes an optimality result for Page's stopping rule in
    quickest change detection. 
## Backtesting & Temporal Correctness

14. **Fonseca, X. (2026).** *Look-Ahead-Freedom as Temporal
    Non-Interference: A Verifiable Correctness Property for Backtesting
    and Agentic Trading Pipelines.* arXiv:2607.04958.\
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04958
    Directly relevant to look-ahead bias, temporal information
    availability, point-in-time data, joins, resampling, and historical
    data retrieval in backtesting pipelines.

## Notes

These references are organized by the research concepts they support
rather than by the order in which they were encountered. They are
intended as the academic foundation for the project's research
directions, not as evidence that any particular strategy is profitable.
